## Design Context

### Users

Hero Forge serves four overlapping player groups: character creators who turn personal images or original characters into playable heroes; strategy players who study BP, roles, skills, positioning, and team counters; collectors who value rarity, provenance, and long-term ownership; and competitive players who care about fair Arena constraints, readable combat, stakes, and rewards. The core job is to forge a hero, understand how it performs, test a formation, and trust the outcome of a formal battle.

### Brand Personality

Dark, tactical, ritualistic, and premium. The interface should feel like a living battle archive and ceremonial command chamber, not a SaaS dashboard or a generic AI character generator. Combat is the emotional centerpiece: tense before impact, decisive during actions, and satisfying at the result.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a dark ritual-metal world with warm aged gold, soot-black stone, worn metal, parchment, smoke, and restrained faction colors. Card art and the battlefield carry the visual weight; UI framing stays legible and subordinate. The standalone Gwent game is the quality reference for battlefield materiality, card presence, readable action focus, and cinematic combat feedback. Avoid generic sci-fi HUDs, glassmorphism, neon cyan/purple AI palettes, repeated dashboard cards, and decorative terminal noise.

### Design Principles

1. Battle is the protagonist: the board, acting unit, target, and consequence must dominate before side panels or logs.
2. Readability precedes spectacle: every animation must explain source, target, type, magnitude, and resulting state.
3. Build materiality, not glow: use layered stone, metal, paper, weathering, light, shadow, and depth instead of relying on borders and bloom.
4. Direct the whole beat: anticipation, action, impact, recovery, and result should form one authored timing system driven by battle events.
5. Preserve tactical trust: formations, initiative, HP/MP, buffs, deaths, and winner states stay inspectable, pausable, step-able, and accessible.
